01 — The library

Every moment, in the order it happened.

A justified grid keeps each photo’s true shape. Glide through years with the scrubber, zoom the grid with a pinch, and open anything in an instant, full screen, in light or dark.

  • {{icon:layers}}Look-alikes, stackedBursts, RAW+JPEG pairs and near-duplicates fold into one, best shot on top.
  • {{icon:album}}Albums, inside albumsPlus smart albums that fill themselves, and the real folders on your disks.
  • {{icon:clock}}Dates you can trustEach photo keeps the local time where it was taken, wherever you are now.
  • {{icon:pencil}}Edits beside the originalCrop, turn, mirror, light and color. The original stays as it was, and undoing an edit brings it back.
  • {{icon:film}}Videos that play anywherePhone HEVC, AV1, MKV and HDR clips get a copy every browser plays. Trim a clip, or keep a single frame as a photo.

02 — Search & Ask

Describe it. There it is.

Search by what’s in the picture, by words written in it, or by the prompt that made it. Or just ask a question. It all runs on your server, so nothing leaves the house to answer it. Places works the same way: each town is named offline, and your photos land on a map.

05 — The household

Everyone’s photos. One home.

Each person keeps a private library, and a shared space gathers what you choose: by hand, or automatically, by who’s in the photo. Share an album with a link or a personal invitation, with no sign-up for the people you send it to.

  • {{icon:household}}Auto-share rules“Photos with the kids, from this day on” goes to the household by itself.

06 — Bringing it in

Every way in.
One library.

Bring years of photos in from wherever they are now, with the dates, albums and prompts that came with them. Photostead keeps one copy of each file, so bringing something in twice does no harm.

{{icon:upload}}

From your browser

Drag files or whole folders onto the page. Uploads are filed by the day they were taken, in folders you can read without Photostead.

{{icon:importIn}}

From Google and Apple

Drop in a Google Takeout or an Apple Photos export. Dates, descriptions and places come along, and Google’s albums and favorites too.

{{icon:phone}}

From your iPhone

Photostead for iPhone backs up your camera roll in the background, iCloud originals included.

{{icon:screen}}

From the web

Photostead Capture, a browser extension, saves posts from X, CivitAI, Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest with their text, prompts and a link back.

{{icon:wand}}

{{icon:spark}}

Privacy

Private by design,
not by promise.

We never see your library, because it never comes to us. There’s no Photostead cloud to sign up for, to trust, or to leak, and the code is open for anyone to check.

{{icon:database}}

Your server, your disks

Photos, faces, search and everything else live on hardware you own, in folders you can read without us.

{{icon:spark}}

AI that works at home

Search by meaning, faces, text in pictures and place names all work locally. A GPU helps; it isn’t required.

{{icon:shield}}

No cloud in the loop

Search, Ask and face recognition are answered by your own server. Nothing about your library goes to an AI service unless you connect your own assistant.

{{icon:eyeOff}}

No ads. No tracking.

The app and this site carry no analytics, advertising or tracking code. There is no account with us to make.

{{icon:lock}}

A vault for the private ones

Behind a PIN, and kept out of the timeline, search, memories and sharing until you open it.

{{icon:image}}

Originals untouched

Files stay byte for byte as they arrived. Stars, keywords, descriptions, names and edits go in XMP sidecars that Lightroom, digiKam and darktable read.

Runs on what you have

One server.
Your hardware.

Photostead runs as a single container on a home server or NAS, or as an app on a Mac or Windows PC, with its database built in. On a server, point it at the folders you already have: it indexes them in place, read-only if you like, and watches them for new files.

UnraidOne container, with your originals on the array under parity DockerOne Compose file for Synology, TrueNAS, Portainer or any Linux box MacAn app in the menu bar, on Apple silicon WindowsAn app in the tray, on 64-bit Windows

Free and open source, under the GNU AGPL. The code is on GitHub.
